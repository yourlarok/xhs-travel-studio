# Card deck design

## Editorial rhythm

A seven-card guide usually works as this arc. Adapt it rather than forcing irrelevant pages.

1. **Cover / feeling:** one photographic scene, compact promise, place and use case.
2. **At a glance:** duration, energy, budget, best time, and who it suits.
3. **Route / sequence:** three to five stops or decisions connected spatially or chronologically.
4. **Hero experience:** a larger scene with one memorable instruction or cultural observation.
5. **Useful language:** one phrase, menu key, address, or interface label that can be shown to someone.
6. **Tradeoff / reality check:** crowds, effort, cost, weather, or an alternative.
7. **Save card:** compact checklist, Chinese names, checked date, and next related guide.

## Vary page composition

Use at least four distinct compositions across seven cards: full-bleed photo with quiet text field; paper-map route with numbered stops; vertical timeline; phrase card with oversized Chinese characters; split photo and practical note; two-column comparison; cost and duration strip; three-item checklist; cultural close-up with a short annotation.

Avoid seven repetitions of a centered heading over a gradient. Text must remain selectable and editable in the layout layer; generated images should not contain important typography.

## Image direction

Use documentary realism unless another direction is requested. Show contemporary local life, useful spatial context, and small details. Avoid generic landmarks on every page, fantasy architecture, costume stereotypes, excessive red lanterns, oversaturated “cyberpunk China,” and scenes that claim to document a real venue without evidence.

Each prompt should name card purpose and subject, city and plausible setting, composition and reserved text area, time of day and emotional register, materials and restrained palette, and exclusions: no text, logo, watermark, fantasy, or stereotypes.

## Type and information hierarchy

- Cover: one short title, one subhead, one small locator.
- Content card: one idea, one headline, up to three short blocks.
- Use large numerals for time, stops, or cost instead of paragraphs.
- Keep copy away from detailed faces and important architecture.
- Use one accent color consistently for route numbers, active states, and key Chinese phrases.
- Let margins and negative space provide calm; do not fill every corner.

## One-guide behavior

The deck should feel continuous when swiped. Repeat a small locator, page number, and accent rule; vary the main composition. A visual motif may travel across pages, such as a thin route line or a red editorial underline.
