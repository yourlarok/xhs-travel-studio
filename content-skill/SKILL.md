# China Inbound Travel Editorial

Create a guide that feels like a thoughtful local friend edited a compact travel magazine. The reader should understand why an experience matters, picture themselves doing it, and know what to do next.

## Start from one traveler decision

Define the reader, travel moment, and decision the guide resolves. Prefer a narrow promise such as “a calm first afternoon after landing” over a broad destination summary. State what the reader will be able to choose, avoid, order, find, or understand after saving the guide.

Build the piece around three forms of value:

- **Desire:** a concrete sensory or cultural reason to care.
- **Confidence:** logistics that remove uncertainty for a visitor who may not speak Chinese.
- **Meaning:** enough context to see the place as more than a photo stop.

## Plan with three traveler coordinates

Every guide plan must define **duration, budget, and execution difficulty** before selecting stops. These coordinates are the reader's fastest way to decide whether a guide fits their day.

- **Duration:** give a realistic door-to-door range, including transfers, queues, meals, and recovery time. Preserve a buffer rather than filling every minute.
- **Budget:** give a per-person RMB range, distinguish required from optional spending, and state whether transport is included.
- **Execution:** rate `easy`, `moderate`, or `complex` from observable friction such as advance bookings, transfers, walking, language dependence, foreign-passport support, and app/payment dependence.

Read [references/guide-planning.md](references/guide-planning.md) when creating, comparing, or scheduling guide plans. Show the three coordinates near the beginning of the guide and preserve them in structured output.

## Research before writing

Verify time-sensitive claims with current authoritative sources where possible. Distinguish official information, firsthand testing, and editorial judgment. Never imply that a flow tested with a Chinese ID or phone number is confirmed for foreign passports or overseas numbers.

Read [references/research-and-trust.md](references/research-and-trust.md) when the guide includes prices, schedules, booking, identity checks, payments, transport, food allergies, safety, or other facts that could disrupt a trip.

## Write with an international editorial voice

Use clear global English with contractions and natural rhythm. Sound observant, warm, and specific. Avoid translation-like headings, tourism-board superlatives, exoticizing language, and slang that assumes one nationality.

Read [references/voice-and-localization.md](references/voice-and-localization.md) for sentence rhythm, transitions, emoji, symbols, Chinese terms, and examples of natural phrasing.

When research includes international traveler reviews, read [references/traveler-voice-synthesis.md](references/traveler-voice-synthesis.md). Learn recurring expectations, vocabulary level, question framing, and tradeoff language without copying a reviewer's distinctive wording or imitating Tripadvisor as a brand voice.

## Compose a visual story

Give every card one job. Alternate scene-setting photography with structured cards such as a route, timeline, decision comparison, phrase card, cost strip, or cultural note. Do not repeat the same title-plus-paragraph layout on every card.

Read [references/card-deck-design.md](references/card-deck-design.md) whenever producing a carousel, poster, infographic, or page-by-page visual plan.

## Return structured production material

For automated or tool-driven production, read [references/output-schema.md](references/output-schema.md) and return its structure. Include image prompts without embedded text; typography and factual overlays belong in the layout layer.

## Editorial quality gate

Before delivery, confirm that:

- the opening creates curiosity without clickbait;
- the piece has one coherent journey rather than a list of facts;
- every logistical detail changes a traveler’s decision or action;
- Chinese names are copyable and translated by function, not only transliterated;
- cultural context explains rather than performs “Chineseness”;
- emoji and symbols guide scanning instead of decorating every line;
- each visual matches the card’s message;
- a reader can identify what to save, screenshot, or show to a local;
- uncertain and time-sensitive claims are labeled with a checked date or source.
- duration, per-person budget, and execution difficulty match the actual route.
